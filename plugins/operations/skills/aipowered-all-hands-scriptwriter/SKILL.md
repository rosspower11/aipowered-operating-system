# All-Hands Scriptwriter

A skill for writing the founder's or CEO's all-hands talk track — the part of the meeting before the team Q&A. Most all-hands open with metrics nobody remembers and end without a story. This skill writes a 20-minute script with a real narrative, named wins, and the awkward truth the team already suspects.

## When to use this skill

- Before a monthly or quarterly all-hands where the leader runs the open
- After a hard week — layoffs, a big miss, a public misstep — and the team needs the truth
- For an offsite kickoff where the leader wants to set the frame
- When the team has stopped engaging in all-hands Q&A — usually because the open is dishonest

## When NOT to use this skill

- For a town hall on a single topic (acquisition, restructure) — that's a different artifact
- For a recorded async update — too short for narrative arc
- For a board or investor talk — the audience is different

## How to run

### Step 1 — Inputs

Pull from the leader:

1. **The frame** — what do you want the team to walk out believing? One sentence.
2. **What's actually true this period** — the win, the miss, the surprise.
3. **The awkward truth** — the thing the team is whispering about that the leader hasn't named.
4. **Named wins** — specific people who shipped specific things. Five max.
5. **What's coming** — the bet for the next 30/60/90 days.

If the leader can't name the awkward truth, find it (Slack rumors, attrition signal, a missed launch). If it's not in the script, the team won't engage.

### Step 2 — Draft a 20-minute script

Three movements, with timing:

**Open: Where we are (5 min)**
- One sentence on what the team already knows.
- The headline metric, in context. Not a slide of charts.
- The frame: "Here's how I'd describe this period in one sentence."

**Middle: The truth (8 min)**
- The win. Name two or three people. Tell the story, not the slide.
- The miss. Own it. Specific cause, specific change. No "we're learning."
- The awkward truth. Name it before the team does. This is the part most leaders skip.

**Close: The bet (7 min)**
- The next 30/60/90. Three priorities. Why these, not others.
- What we're not doing. The cuts matter more than the adds.
- One thing the leader is asking the team to do this week.

End with a question for the Q&A: "What am I missing?"

### Step 3 — Slide outline

Six slides, max:

1. Title + frame sentence
2. Headline metric (one number, in context)
3. Wins (names + ships)
4. Miss + change
5. The bet (3 priorities)
6. The ask + the question

If the deck has more than six, the talk is hiding behind it.

### Step 4 — Voice rules

- First person plural for the company, first person singular for the leader's calls. "I decided" beats "we decided" when it's owned.
- Past tense for what happened, present for what's true now, future tense only for the bet.
- Names in wins. "The team shipped" is invisible.
- No corporate filler: "exciting", "incredible journey", "as you know."
- The awkward truth gets one paragraph, not one line. The team can tell when it's been minimized.

## Hand-off

End with the Slack message the leader sends the morning of the all-hands: `All-hands at {time}. Here's the frame: {one sentence}. Bring questions.` Offer to draft a short follow-up note that ships the next day with the three priorities and the named asks.
