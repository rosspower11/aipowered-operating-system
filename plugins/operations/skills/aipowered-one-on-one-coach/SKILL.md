# 1:1 Coach

A skill for prepping a 1:1 that's actually worth the half hour. Most 1:1s decay into status updates because no one prepared. This skill writes a prep doc that pulls the conversation toward the real material — what's blocking them, what they're growing into, and what they haven't said out loud.

## When to use this skill

- Before a weekly or biweekly 1:1 with a direct report
- Before a skip-level where you need a structure but not a script
- When a 1:1 has gone stale — same updates, no signal, no progress
- Before a new-manager 1:1 where you haven't built trust yet

## When NOT to use this skill

- For a performance conversation — that's a different artifact (PIP, formal review)
- For a status update masquerading as a 1:1 — kill the meeting instead
- For a peer 1:1 — the power dynamic is different, the prompts don't fit

## How to run

### Step 1 — Inputs

Pull from the manager:

1. **Who** — name, role, tenure, reporting line.
2. **Last 1:1 takeaways** — what they were working on, what they were stuck on.
3. **Recent context** — anything happening for them this week (a launch, a missed deadline, a new project).
4. **Manager's read** — one sentence on how the manager thinks the report is doing right now.

### Step 2 — Draft the prep doc

Three sections, in this order:

**1. The check-in (5 min)**
Two prompts max. Open-ended. No "how's it going."
- "What's taken the most energy this week — and was it worth it?"
- "What's one thing that surprised you — good or bad?"

**2. The work (15 min)**
Pull from last 1:1 + recent context. Write three prompts that get past the status update:
- "What's the thing you've been avoiding?"
- "Where are you stuck and pretending you're not?"
- "What would you do differently on {recent project} if you ran it again?"

Pick the one that fits. Don't use all three.

**3. Growth + the unsaid (10 min)**
- A growth prompt tied to their direction: "You said you wanted to {X}. What's the smallest next step?"
- An unsaid prompt: "What's something you haven't told me but probably should?"

### Step 3 — Manager notes (private)

Add a section the manager keeps for themselves:

- One thing to praise specifically (with the receipt — what they did, when).
- One piece of feedback to deliver, sized for this conversation.
- One question the manager is genuinely curious about.

### Step 4 — Rules

- The report drives the agenda; the manager brings prompts, not topics.
- Status updates belong in Slack or a doc. If you find yourself asking "how's the project going," cut it.
- Names in feedback, not "the team." Specific behavior, specific impact.
- No "great question" filler. Listen instead.
- End every 1:1 with: "What do you want me to do differently?"

## Hand-off

End with a short follow-up template the manager can send after the 1:1: `Two things from today: {commitment from manager}, {commitment from report}. Next 1:1: {date}.` Offer to log open threads from this 1:1 to surface in the next prep doc.
