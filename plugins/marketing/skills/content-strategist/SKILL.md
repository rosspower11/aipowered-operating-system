# Content Strategist

A skill for turning a product, audience, and business goal into a sharp, prioritised 90-day content plan. Use this when you have a blank editorial calendar and need a place to start, or when an existing calendar has drifted into "whatever the team felt like writing."

## When to use this skill

- A founder or marketer asks "what should we write?"
- An existing content engine is producing volume but no leads, no rankings, or no shares
- A new launch needs a content arc that builds toward it
- The team is repeating itself and topics need to be re-clustered

## When NOT to use this skill

- For one-off pieces (use a single prompt — this is overkill for a single blog post)
- For pure SEO keyword expansion (use the SEO Headline Generator prompt or a dedicated SEO tool)
- When the user has not yet defined audience or product clearly — push back and ask first

## How to run

Work in three passes. Don't skip ahead.

### Pass 1 — Inputs

Ask the user for the following, all at once. Don't drip-feed questions.

1. **Product** — what it is, who it's for, what it replaces, what makes it different
2. **Audience** — role, seniority, where they hang out, what they read, what they Google
3. **Business goal for this quarter** — pipeline, signups, ranking for X, brand authority, etc.
4. **Constraints** — channels they refuse to use, tone limits, existing content to build on
5. **Cadence** — how many pieces per week, who's writing, who's editing

If any answer is vague ("we want growth"), name the vagueness back and ask for the specific version.

### Pass 2 — Strategy

Produce a one-page strategy with:

- **Positioning sentence** — one line: who it's for, what it does, what it replaces
- **Three content pillars** — themes the brand will be known for. Each pillar has a one-sentence promise and three sub-topics underneath.
- **Format mix** — what % of output is long-form, social, lifecycle, video, etc., and why for this audience
- **One arc** — how the 90 days build toward the quarter goal (e.g. "weeks 1-4 educate, 5-8 case studies, 9-12 launch")

### Pass 3 — Calendar

A 12-week table with one row per week and columns: `week`, `pillar`, `format`, `working title`, `angle`, `target keyword (if SEO)`, `CTA`. Every row is concrete enough that a writer could brief it tomorrow.

## Examples of strong vs weak output

**Weak**: "Pillar 1: Productivity. Topics: time management, focus, deep work."
**Strong**: "Pillar 1: How senior ICs ship without becoming managers. Promise: every post shows a real workflow. Sub-topics: async standups, decision logs, calendar archaeology."

**Weak**: "Week 3: blog post about onboarding."
**Strong**: "Week 3 — pillar: ICs shipping. Format: long-form (1,500w). Title: 'The 90-day plan I give every new staff engineer'. Angle: contrarian — most onboarding plans are too vague. Keyword: staff engineer onboarding. CTA: download the plan template."

## Hand-off

When the calendar is done, summarise: pillars, arc, first three weeks of titles. Offer to draft the first brief if the user wants to keep going. Don't keep planning past 90 days — anything further is fiction at this stage.
