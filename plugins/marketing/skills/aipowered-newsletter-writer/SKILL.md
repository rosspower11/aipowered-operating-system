# Newsletter Writer

A skill for turning a Slack-dumped pile of wins, links, and half-thoughts into a weekly newsletter with a personality. Use this when a founder or operator already has the inputs but no time or instinct to write the email, or when an existing newsletter has decayed into a list of links nobody opens.

## When to use this skill

- A weekly cadence exists and inputs are flowing but the draft never gets written
- Open rates have slipped under 30% and the format is roundup-shaped
- A new newsletter is launching and needs a repeatable structure
- A founder wants to write in their own voice but keeps starting over

## When NOT to use this skill

- For a one-time launch announcement (use a campaign brief, not a newsletter format)
- For transactional or lifecycle email sequences (different muscle, different brief)
- When the inputs are empty — refuse to invent stories, ask for raw material

## How to run

Work in three steps. The whole thing should take 25 minutes from inputs to send.

### Step 1 — Triage the dump

Take the raw input pile (Slack messages, links, screenshots, quotes). Sort into:

- **One lead story** — something that happened, was learned, or was decided. 250–400 words.
- **Three secondary items** — links, quotes, or one-liners. 40–60 words each.
- **One ask or CTA** — reply, book, click, share. Single, never multiple.

If there's no lead story, the email is a roundup and roundups die. Tell the writer and stop until they have one.

### Step 2 — Write the lead like a personal email

The lead is the entire reason people subscribe. Rules:

- First line is a scene, not a meta-comment ("Yesterday at 2am the deploy failed" beats "Hope you had a great week")
- Second person and first person, never third. "I shipped this. Here's what broke. You'll hit the same wall."
- One concrete number minimum: a date, a metric, a count
- One opinion stated plainly, not hedged
- Land on a transferable lesson — what the reader does differently on Monday

The lead is a story with a takeaway, not a takeaway with a story bolted on.

### Step 3 — Build the rest, fast

Structure:

1. **Subject line** — under 50 characters, curiosity over information. Test 2 variants.
2. **Preview text** — extends the subject, doesn't repeat it. Under 90 characters.
3. **Lead story** — from Step 2.
4. **Three secondaries** — bullet list, each: bold mini-headline, one-line tease, link. No commentary longer than the tease.
5. **One CTA** — bottom of email, one button, one verb. "Reply with X" beats "Check out our latest."
6. **Sign-off** — first name. No corporate sign-off, no "the team."

Total length: 500–800 words. Anything longer and the second story dies in the scroll.

## Hand-off

Deliver subject lines (2), preview text, and the full email in one block. Flag any input that was too thin to use, so next week's dump can be richer. Offer the next week's lead-story prompt if a thread is forming. Don't pre-write four weeks ahead — newsletters live or die on this week's reality.
