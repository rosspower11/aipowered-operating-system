# Long-form Writer

A skill for turning a one-page brief into a 1,500-word article that reads like a person wrote it. Use this when a writer or founder has a thesis and source material but no draft yet, or when an existing draft is generic, hedged, and could have been written by anyone in the category.

## When to use this skill

- A brief exists and a publishable draft is needed in one pass
- A founder has a sharp opinion but no time to write
- A previous draft is full of hedges, listicles, and "today's fast-paced world" filler
- A piece needs to anchor a content pillar and stand on its own without other links

## When NOT to use this skill

- For a 300-word newsletter section (use the Newsletter Writer skill)
- For social posts or threads (use LinkedIn Post Writer or Twitter Thread Architect)
- When no thesis exists yet — push back and demand one before writing

## How to run

Work in four steps. Don't skip ahead, even if the brief looks complete.

### Step 1 — Pressure-test the thesis

Read the brief. State the thesis back in one sentence: "This piece argues X because Y." If you can't, the brief is too vague — ask the writer for the one-line argument and the strongest reason it's true. No thesis, no draft.

### Step 2 — Outline in 7 beats

Write a seven-beat skeleton, no longer:

1. Hook — a specific scene, number, or claim. Not "in today's world."
2. The setup — what most people believe or do
3. The crack — where that belief breaks, with a real example
4. The thesis — stated plainly
5. The mechanism — why it works, with a named framework or sequence
6. The proof — one case study, one dataset, or one named example
7. The ask — a single CTA

Each beat gets ~200 words. That's your 1,400, plus a 100-word intro buffer.

### Step 3 — Draft, in voice

Write straight through. Rules:

- One idea per paragraph. Three sentences average, never more than five.
- Concrete nouns and verbs. "The CEO killed the launch" beats "leadership made the decision to deprioritise."
- Real numbers from the brief — never made up. If the brief lacks them, mark `[NEEDS NUMBER]` and move on.
- One named example minimum. "Linear", "Stripe's docs team", "the 2019 Wirecutter relaunch" — not "a SaaS company."
- Forbidden phrases: leverage, synergy, operationalise, unlock, seamless, robust, "in today's fast-paced world." Strip them on sight.

### Step 4 — Cut 15%

First drafts are 15% too long. Read top to bottom, delete every sentence that restates the previous one, every adverb that isn't load-bearing, every transition like "moreover" or "furthermore." End the piece on the strongest sentence — usually two paragraphs before the writer thinks they're done.

## Hand-off

Deliver the 1,500-word draft, the 7-beat outline above it for the editor, and a list of any `[NEEDS NUMBER]` gaps. Offer to write a 280-character pull-quote and a meta description next. Don't write a second draft unsolicited — let the editor cut first.
