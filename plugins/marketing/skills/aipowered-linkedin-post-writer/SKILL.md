# LinkedIn Post Writer

A skill for writing one LinkedIn post that sounds like a real operator, not a ghostwriter. Use this when a founder has a story or opinion but the draft they wrote sounds generic, or when a marketing team is posting on a founder's behalf and the voice keeps slipping into corporate gloss.

## When to use this skill

- A founder has a specific moment, decision, or opinion to share
- A team is running a personal-brand cadence and needs one post for the day
- A draft exists but is hedged, list-heavy, or full of LinkedIn-isms
- A launch needs a founder post that doesn't sound like the company blog

## When NOT to use this skill

- For company-page posts (different voice, different rules)
- For carousels (use the carousel format inside Content Repurposer)
- When the founder has nothing real to say — refuse to invent. Bad founder content is worse than no founder content.

## How to run

Work in three steps. The whole post should land between 150 and 300 words.

### Step 1 — Find the moment

Ask the founder for one of three things:

- **A decision they made this month** that someone else would have made differently
- **A mistake they made** and what they'd do over
- **A pattern they're seeing** across their last 5 customer conversations

If they offer a "thought leadership" abstract take with no anchor, push back: "What happened that made you think that?" The post needs a moment, not a manifesto.

### Step 2 — Write three hooks, one body

Three hook options, one of which the founder will pick:

1. **Scene hook** — "Last Tuesday, [specific thing happened]." Concrete, dated.
2. **Counter hook** — "Most people think X. They're wrong, and here's the data." Picks a fight.
3. **Number hook** — "We just did [specific result]. Here's what changed." Earns the read with proof.

Then one body, structured as:

- **Line 1** — the hook (above)
- **Lines 2–4** — the setup, one sentence each, double-spaced
- **The pivot** — "Here's what I learned" or "Here's what nobody tells you"
- **The lesson** — 2–4 short paragraphs, one idea each
- **The CTA** — one ask. "What would you have done?" beats "Hit follow for more."

### Step 3 — Strip the LinkedIn-isms

Read the draft. Delete on sight:

- Emoji bullet points (the rocket-ship-checkmark theatre)
- "Here are 5 lessons I learned" lists
- "Agree?" as a closing
- "Synergy", "leverage", "unlock", "operationalise", "game-changer"
- Hashtags more than three. Three is the cap. One is better.

Replace the deleted lines with one specific detail — a name, a number, a quote. Specificity is the entire game on LinkedIn.

## Hand-off

Deliver three hook variants and one full post body. Mark which hook you'd ship and why in one line. Offer to write a follow-up reply for when the founder gets engagement. Don't write a week of posts in advance — LinkedIn voice goes stale fast.
