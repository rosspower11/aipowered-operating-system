# Content Repurposer

A skill for taking one published long-form piece and turning it into the rest of the week's content — five social posts, one newsletter section, and one short-form video script. Use this when a strong article is sitting alone with no distribution arc, or when the team is producing one big thing per week and nothing else.

## When to use this skill

- A 1,500–3,000 word article just shipped and needs a distribution week behind it
- The team is publishing weekly but social and lifecycle channels are starving
- A founder wrote one banger and wants 30 days of derivative content from it
- An evergreen piece is being relaunched and needs fresh creative

## When NOT to use this skill

- For a piece under 800 words — there isn't enough substance to atomise
- When the source piece is weak — repurposing bad content multiplies bad content
- For platform-native content that was never meant to be cross-posted (a Twitter thread back to LinkedIn rarely works)

## How to run

Work in three steps. Each output gets channel-native treatment — no copy-paste.

### Step 1 — Extract the spine

Read the source. Pull out:

- **The thesis** — one sentence
- **3–5 atomic claims** — standalone arguments that survive without the full piece
- **Every concrete asset** — numbers, frameworks, named examples, quotes, screenshots
- **The reader transformation** — what they believed before vs after

If you can't find 3 atomic claims, the source is thinner than it looked. Tell the user and stop.

### Step 2 — Map to channels

Build the asset list before writing:

| Channel | Format | Job to do |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Founder-voice post, 200–300 words | Drive saves and comments |
| LinkedIn | Carousel, 7–10 slides | Drive shares |
| X | Single tweet with image | Drive likes and quote-tweets |
| X | 8-tweet thread | Drive replies and follows |
| Instagram/TikTok | 45-second talking-head script | Drive watch-time |
| Newsletter | 250-word section + link | Drive click-through |

Each pulls from a different atomic claim. Never reuse the same claim twice — the audience overlaps and they will notice.

### Step 3 — Write channel-native, not channel-translated

Rules per channel:

- **LinkedIn post**: hook in first line, no link until comment one, one personal beat
- **LinkedIn carousel**: one idea per slide, slide 1 is the hook, last slide is the CTA, no walls of text
- **Tweet**: 240 characters max, one image, no thread bait
- **Thread**: 8 tweets, each earning the next read (see Twitter Thread Architect)
- **Video script**: 0–3s hook, 3–35s payoff, 35–45s CTA. Talking-head, no B-roll dependency
- **Newsletter section**: tease the claim, deliver the punchline, link out for the full argument

A correct repurpose makes each post stronger than the source paragraph it came from.

## Hand-off

Deliver all 7 assets in one document, ordered by ship day across the week with suggested send times. Flag which atomic claim each one uses so nothing repeats. Offer to draft the next week's set from the same source if it has more spine. Stop at week two — beyond that the piece is fully atomised.
