# Twitter Thread Architect

A skill for turning one sharp idea into a 7–12 tweet thread that gets read top to bottom. Use this when a founder or writer has a strong claim and needs to expand it into the format that still drives the most followers and replies on X, or when an existing thread is good for the first three tweets and then loses people.

## When to use this skill

- A long-form piece exists and needs a thread version that stands alone
- A founder has a contrarian take they want to test before writing a full essay
- A launch needs a thread that builds toward the announcement without leaking it early
- A previous thread had a great hook but read-through fell off after tweet 3

## When NOT to use this skill

- For single tweets (different muscle — one-liners are not threads compressed)
- When the idea is thin enough to fit in one tweet — don't pad
- For threads chasing engagement-bait formats ("a thread on 7 tools you must use") — they decay the account

## How to run

Work in four steps. Total time: 30 minutes for a sharp thread.

### Step 1 — Lock the core idea

State the thread in one sentence: "This thread argues X." If it takes two sentences, you have two threads. Pick one.

The strongest threads have a single named claim, a contrarian or counterintuitive angle, and a concrete proof. If any of those three is missing, the thread is mid before you write tweet one.

### Step 2 — Architect the spine

A 9-tweet skeleton works for most threads:

1. **Hook** — the claim, sharpened. Three patterns to test: counter ("Everyone says X. Here's why X is wrong."), promise ("In 5 minutes I'll show you how Y."), scene ("Last year I lost $40k on Z. Here's what I learned.")
2. **Stakes** — why this matters in one tweet
3. **The setup** — what most people do or believe
4. **The crack** — where it breaks
5. **The thesis** — your claim, plainly stated
6. **The mechanism** — why your claim works
7. **The proof** — one example, with numbers
8. **The application** — what the reader does on Monday
9. **The CTA** — one ask. "Bookmark this", "Reply with your version", "Follow for more X" — pick one, never stack.

Stretch to 12 tweets only if proof needs three examples, not one.

### Step 3 — Write each tweet to earn the next

Rules per tweet:

- 240 characters max — leaves room for re-quotes
- Every tweet ends on tension or curiosity, never resolution. The exception is the final CTA.
- No "1/" numbering — it telegraphs length and kills the hook
- Line breaks every 1–2 sentences for mobile scan
- One image, chart, or screenshot in tweet 5 or 6 — pure-text threads cap on engagement

### Step 4 — Pressure-test the hook

Read tweet 1 cold. Would you click "show this thread" if you'd never heard of the author? If no, the hook isn't earning. Three common failures:

- **Throat-clearing** — "I've been thinking about X lately." Cut it.
- **Generic claim** — "Marketing is changing." Make it specific or kill it.
- **Spoiling the punchline** — "The answer is to ship faster." Save it for tweet 5.

## Hand-off

Deliver the 7–12 tweet thread, three hook variants for tweet 1, and a one-line note on which hook to ship. Offer to write the quote-tweet boost for 24 hours later. Don't write a follow-up thread until this one has run for 72 hours and you've seen what landed.
