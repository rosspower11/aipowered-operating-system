# Brand Voice Codifier

A skill for taking 5–10 pieces of existing writing — blog posts, founder tweets, sales emails — and producing a voice guide a new writer can actually use. Use this when a brand has good writing but no documented voice, or when an existing voice doc is full of adjectives ("we are bold, friendly, and authentic") that nobody can apply to a real sentence.

## When to use this skill

- A new writer is joining and the existing voice doc is useless
- An agency or freelancer needs a reference to match in-house tone
- A brand is scaling content output and drift has started showing
- A founder writes well but can't articulate why — and others are now writing for them

## When NOT to use this skill

- With fewer than 5 samples — too thin to extract patterns
- When samples were written by 5+ different people without a unified voice yet (define the voice first, then codify)
- For pure visual brand work (this is voice only, not logo/typography)

## How to run

Work in four steps. Output is a one-page guide, not a 30-page brand bible.

### Step 1 — Read like an editor

Read all samples back-to-back. Mark in three colours:

- **Green** — sentences that sound unmistakably like the brand. Save them.
- **Yellow** — sentences that work but could be anyone's. Note them.
- **Red** — sentences that sound off-brand even if the writer didn't catch it. Note them.

Aim for 15–20 green sentences across the samples. If you can't find that many, the voice isn't consistent yet.

### Step 2 — Extract patterns

Across the green sentences, look for repeats on five dimensions:

1. **Sentence length** — average words per sentence, and the rhythm pattern (short-short-long, long-medium-short)
2. **Vocabulary** — words used 3+ times. Note both the loved words and the avoided ones (does the brand say "customer" or "user" or "operator"?).
3. **Person and tense** — first/second/third, present/past
4. **Humour register** — dry, none, self-deprecating, observational
5. **Rhetorical moves** — does the brand use questions, contrast, lists, repetition?

Quote the green sentences as proof under each pattern.

### Step 3 — Define the forbidden list

This is the highest-value section and most voice docs skip it. List:

- **Forbidden words** — 10–20 words this brand never uses. Be specific: "leverage", "best-in-class", "robust", "synergy", "in today's fast-paced world."
- **Forbidden moves** — emoji bullet lists, exclamation marks, hashtag stacks, "Here are 5 lessons" listicles, etc. — whatever the green samples never do.
- **Forbidden positioning** — claims this brand never makes (e.g. "the only", "AI-powered" if it isn't, "revolutionary").

A voice is more defined by what it refuses than what it embraces.

### Step 4 — Write the one-pager

Structure:

- **Voice in one sentence** — "We sound like a [role] who has [credential] and is talking to [audience]."
- **Three voice principles** — each with a green-sentence example and a red-sentence rewrite
- **Vocabulary** — preferred terms, mapped from common alternatives
- **Rhythm rules** — sentence length and pattern guidance
- **Forbidden list** — words, moves, positioning
- **Decision rules** — three "when in doubt" rules a writer applies in real time

## Hand-off

Deliver the one-page voice guide and a 50-line "before/after" rewrite section showing red sentences fixed into green. Offer to test the guide on a new draft from a different writer. Update the guide every 6 months — voice drifts and new samples surface new patterns.
