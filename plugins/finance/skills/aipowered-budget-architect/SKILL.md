# Budget Architect

A skill for turning take-home pay and three months of bank statements into a budget you'll actually follow. Most budgets fail because they're aspirational fiction — 40 categories, no slack, no review ritual. This one is built to survive month two: fixed costs locked in, flex spend bounded, and a 20-minute monthly check-in that catches drift before it becomes denial.

## When to use this skill

- Someone has never budgeted and wants a starting structure that isn't 40 categories deep
- A household is fighting about money and needs a shared system, not another app
- A budget exists on paper but the bank balance keeps disagreeing with it
- Income just changed (raise, new job, partner moved in) and the old plan is stale

## When NOT to use this skill

- For business cashflow — use the Runway Modeller instead
- For debt payoff strategy — pair with the Debt Payoff Strategist; this skill assumes debt has a plan already
- When the user has not pulled their last 90 days of transactions — push back and ask first

## How to run

Work in three phases. Don't skip phase 1 — the rest is fiction without it.

### Phase 1 — Ground truth (the actual numbers)

Ask for:

1. **Net monthly take-home** (after tax, after 401(k), after health insurance) — the number that hits the bank
2. **Last 90 days of spend** exported from their bank or Monarch/Copilot/YNAB
3. **Fixed obligations** — rent/mortgage, debt minimums, insurance, subscriptions over $10/mo
4. **Goals with dollar amounts** — emergency fund target, savings rate, one specific thing they're saving for

Categorise the 90 days into: **Fixed** (same every month), **Flex** (groceries, gas, utilities — variable but necessary), **Discretionary** (eating out, shopping, entertainment), **Goals** (savings, investments, debt payoff above minimum). If discretionary is more than 30% of take-home, name it directly.

### Phase 2 — The plan

Build a monthly budget using a modified 50/30/20 — but use their real fixed costs, not the textbook split. Structure:

- **Fixed**: whatever their actual fixed is (often 55-65% in HCOL areas — that's fine, name it)
- **Flex**: capped, with weekly grocery and gas limits
- **Discretionary**: a single "fun money" pool — don't subdivide into 8 categories nobody tracks
- **Goals**: at least 15% of take-home, automated on payday before anything else moves

If there's not enough room for goals, the answer is reduce discretionary or earn more — don't shave flex below realistic. Underbudgeting groceries is the #1 reason budgets blow up in week three.

### Phase 3 — The ritual

A budget without review is a wish. Set up:

- **Weekly 5-minute check** — open the app, scan flex and discretionary, no judgment
- **Monthly 20-minute review** — last day of the month: actual vs. plan, what got missed (annual costs hit monthly), one thing to tweak
- **Quarterly recalibration** — adjust caps based on three months of real data

## Hand-off

Summarise the budget as one page: take-home, four buckets with dollar amounts, the goal automation, and the date of the first monthly review. Offer to design the savings automation rules next. This is general guidance, not personalised financial, tax, or legal advice — for big moves, talk to a licensed advisor.
