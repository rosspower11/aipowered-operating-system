# Debt Payoff Strategist

A skill for taking a messy list of debts — credit cards, student loans, car notes, that one IRS payment plan — and producing a single plan with a payoff date. Most people stall because they don't know which debt to attack first or how much extra to throw at it. This skill picks the method, runs the numbers, and writes the first 90 days down.

## When to use this skill

- Someone has 2+ debts and no clear order to attack them in
- Minimums are being paid but balances barely move
- A windfall (bonus, tax refund, sale of something) is about to land and needs a job
- A previous payoff plan stalled out and motivation has cratered

## When NOT to use this skill

- When monthly cashflow can't cover minimums — fix income or expenses first
- For mortgage payoff strategy alone — usually not the priority above 4% other debt
- For business debt — use the Runway Modeller and Business Tax Strategist

## How to run

Work in four steps. Don't skip step 3 — that's where the plan becomes real.

### Step 1 — Inventory

Get every debt in one table with: balance, APR, minimum payment, lender. Include: credit cards, personal loans, student loans, car loans, BNPL, family loans, IRS/state tax debt, medical. Don't include: mortgage (separate conversation). Total it.

Also get: monthly take-home, total monthly minimums, and **extra dollars available for debt** after a realistic budget. If the answer is "zero," stop and run the Budget Architect first.

### Step 2 — Pick the method

Two methods, one decision:

- **Avalanche** — pay minimums on everything, throw extra at highest APR first. Mathematically optimal. Saves the most interest.
- **Snowball** — pay minimums on everything, throw extra at smallest balance first. Psychologically optimal. You feel wins faster.

Default to avalanche if the highest APR is above 18% — credit card interest compounds brutally. Default to snowball if the user has stalled out before, has 5+ debts, or describes themselves as "overwhelmed." Snowball beats avalanche if you've quit twice already — a slower payoff you finish beats a faster one you abandon.

### Step 3 — Run the numbers

Build a payoff schedule:

- Month-by-month for the first 12 months
- Show: target debt, extra payment, balance after, total debt remaining
- When a debt is killed, roll its full payment (minimum + extra) into the next target
- Calculate the **payoff date** for the full stack and **total interest saved** vs. minimums-only

Name a number. "Debt-free by November 2028" is what people remember. "It depends" is not a plan.

### Step 4 — First 90 days

Concrete actions:

1. **This week** — automate minimums, set up the extra payment to the target debt on payday
2. **Day 30** — check balance, confirm one debt got smaller, no balance transfer regret
3. **Day 60** — if a 0% balance transfer makes sense (good credit, no transfer fee that eats the savings), run it
4. **Day 90** — first milestone check, adjust if income changed

Flag the trap: don't open new credit while paying down old. Don't consolidate into a longer term to feel better — that's just cosmetics.

## Hand-off

Deliver the inventory table, the chosen method with reasoning, the payoff date, and the first 90 days of action. Offer to revisit at day 90. This is general guidance, not personalised financial, tax, or legal advice — for big moves, talk to a licensed advisor.
