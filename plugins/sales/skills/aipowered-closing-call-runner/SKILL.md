# Closing Call Runner

A skill for the call where the deal actually closes — or doesn't. Use this when the rep has a buyer agreed on value, a verbal yes from the champion, and the next 30 minutes will decide whether paper goes out this week. Output is a minute-by-minute plan covering recap, the price reveal, the objection branches, and the move that gets a signature timeline on the call.

## When to use this skill

- The verbal "we want to move forward" has happened, and the closing call is next
- Late-stage deal where the rep needs to bring procurement into the room without losing momentum
- A deal that has slipped one quarter and the rep cannot afford to slip another
- Rep is closing their first deal over $100k and needs structure, not improv
- After a competitive bake-off where the buyer needs to be walked into the choice

## When NOT to use this skill

- The buyer has not yet given a verbal commitment to value or fit — go back to discovery
- Pure exploratory calls — this is a closing skill, not a presentation skill
- For deals where the buyer has hard-pivoted to "send me a contract and I'll think about it" — different motion (proposal-led, not call-led)

## How to run

### Phase 1 — Pre-call assembly (same day)

Lock four things before the call:
1. **Attendee list** — economic buyer in the room, not "joining for the last 5 minutes"
2. **Mutual close plan** — already shared, already redlined; print the calendar dates
3. **The number** — final price, discount lever, and the floor (what the rep will not go below)
4. **Paper status** — MSA template ready, security questionnaire pre-filled

If any of these is unresolved, the call is not a closing call. Reschedule it.

### Phase 2 — The 30-minute structure

| Minutes | Section | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Reset the room | Recap value, recap timeline, confirm EB is here for the decision |
| 3–10 | Recap of agreed value | Repeat the buyer's metrics and pain back to them, in their words |
| 10–15 | Reveal the number | Price, terms, what's included — slow, no apology |
| 15–25 | Objection cycle | Buyer pushes back; rep runs the playbook |
| 25–28 | Confirm path to signature | Specific dates: redlines back, security done, signature target |
| 28–30 | Mutual commitment | Rep states what they'll do; ask buyer to state what they'll do |

### Phase 3 — The price reveal

One sentence. Anchored. No "so I think we'd be looking at around…". State the number, state what's included, then stop talking. The first person who fills the silence loses leverage. Coach the rep to count to ten.

### Phase 4 — Objection branches (pre-loaded, not improvised)

Prep three responses, ready before the call:

- **Price** → reframe to outcome, then offer one trade (term length, payment terms) — never a discount without a trade
- **Timing** → cost-of-inaction in their numbers (the metric they shared in discovery), pilot terms as fallback
- **Competition** → name it, name the trade-off honestly, return to the buyer's stated criteria

Never use the word "concern" as a euphemism for objection. Call it what it is.

### Phase 5 — Signature push

End with a date, an attendee, and an action. Examples: "Redlines back from your legal by Thursday, mutual sign by next Friday." If the buyer dodges the date, that's the real objection — surface it and run the branch.

## Hand-off

Output the minute-by-minute call plan, the exact price-reveal sentence, the three objection branches with rebuttals, and the close question the rep will ask at minute 28. End with the one fallback ask if the buyer pushes signature out — a signed proof-of-intent or pilot SOW, never a vague "let's talk next week."
