# Proposal Writer

A skill for writing the proposal that closes the deal — not the 14-page PDF the buyer will skim once and forget. Use this when a rep has run discovery, agreed value with the champion, and now needs a document that the EB can sign off on in under 4 minutes of reading. Output is a 2-page proposal (or a tight Notion/Doc) with scope, price, outcomes, and a single decision at the end.

## When to use this skill

- Late-stage deal where the buyer has asked "send me something to share internally"
- Procurement-driven cycle where the proposal is what gets passed up the chain to the EB
- Renewals or expansions where the existing customer wants a written re-baseline
- Founder-led deals where the founder is writing the proposal at midnight and it shows
- Multi-stakeholder deals where the proposal will be the only artifact the absent EB ever sees

## When NOT to use this skill

- The buyer hasn't agreed on the problem yet — a proposal won't fix that, more discovery will
- Self-serve / PLG motions — a proposal is overkill, send a checkout link
- For RFP responses — different format, different rules, different skill

## How to run

### Phase 1 — Inputs (no blanks tolerated)

Collect:
1. **Buyer outcome** — quoted from discovery, in their words, with a number attached
2. **Current state cost** — what staying still costs them, also in a number
3. **Scope** — exactly what the rep is selling: modules, seat count, services
4. **Price** — list, discount, term, payment terms
5. **Timeline** — kickoff date, time-to-first-value, full-rollout date
6. **Decision date** — when the EB needs this signed by

If any field is "TBD", stop. The proposal will be soft and the deal will slip. Go back to the buyer for the missing piece before drafting.

### Phase 2 — Structure (2 pages, in this order)

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **The outcome** | 3–4 sentences | What the buyer gets, in the buyer's metric |
| **What we're delivering** | Bulleted scope | Specific, named, quantified — no "support" without a tier |
| **Investment** | Table | Price, term, payment, what's included, what's add-on |
| **Timeline** | 4-row table | Kickoff, first value, milestone, full deployment |
| **Why us / why now** | 4–6 sentences | One named customer with a quantified result similar to theirs |
| **Decision** | Single line | One signature block, one date |

### Phase 3 — Language rules

Ban these:
- "Best-in-class", "industry-leading", "world-class" (mean nothing, signal vendor)
- "Solutions" as a noun (everyone has solutions)
- "We are pleased to submit" (no, you're trying to close)
- Executive summary at the front (the EB reads two paragraphs; make those the outcome and the price, not throat-clearing)

Use the buyer's exact words for their problem. If they said "our reps are burning 4 hours a day on data entry", the proposal says that — not "operational inefficiencies."

### Phase 4 — The outcomes contract

Every proposal lists 3 named outcomes the buyer will see in 90 days, each with a measurable target. Not "improved efficiency" — "reduce data-entry time per rep from 4 hours/day to under 1 hour/day by day 60." Write outcomes the rep would stake their renewal on, because they will.

### Phase 5 — Pressure test before sending

Three checks:
1. Could the EB sign this in under 5 minutes of reading?
2. Is there a single, dated decision at the end?
3. Would a competitor's proposal look the same? If yes, the buyer-specific outcomes aren't sharp enough — rewrite.

## Hand-off

Deliver as a clean 2-page document the rep can paste into Docs or PandaDoc. End with the one specific clause that will trigger the buyer's procurement team (term length, auto-renew, payment terms) so the rep is ready for the redline before it lands.
