# Discovery Call Prepper

A skill for prepping a 30-minute first call with a real buyer in the room. Use this when the rep has 30 minutes between meetings and a discovery on the calendar with a named account they haven't fully researched. Output is a one-page brief plus a question script ordered by what the rep needs to learn, not what they want to pitch.

## When to use this skill

- 24–48 hours before a first discovery call with a qualified inbound or outbound prospect
- Before a "second first call" where the rep is meeting a new stakeholder at an existing account
- Onboarding a new AE who needs a repeatable prep ritual, not vibes
- Before a call where the prospect's title is senior (VP+) and there's only one shot at a follow-up
- Re-prepping a stalled deal where the rep is about to get back in front of the buyer cold

## When NOT to use this skill

- For a routine demo with no new stakeholder — different skill, different goals
- Pure qualification calls under 15 minutes (BANT-style triage) — overkill
- When the call is in 20 minutes — there's no time, just read the LinkedIn and go

## How to run

### Phase 1 — Account research (10 minutes of work, max)

Pull from public sources:

1. **Company** — last funding round, headcount trajectory, last 3 press mentions
2. **The buyer** — title, tenure, last LinkedIn post, prior companies, any podcast or conference appearance
3. **The team** — who reports to the buyer, recent hires (job-post archaeology), recent departures
4. **Tech stack** — what's in BuiltWith / job posts that hints at what they use today

If any of those fields come back blank, flag it. A blank "tenure" is a tell — don't go into the call without knowing if the buyer started last week.

### Phase 2 — Hypothesis drafting

Write a 2-sentence hypothesis: what is the buyer's most likely top-3 priority this quarter, and which one does the product touch? If the answer is "all three," the hypothesis is too soft — pick one.

### Phase 3 — Question script (the 30-minute structure)

Lay it out like this:

| Minutes | Section | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Rapport + agenda lock | Confirm time and what success looks like |
| 3–10 | Current state | What they do today, what's broken |
| 10–18 | Future state + cost of inaction | What they want, what it costs to stay still |
| 18–24 | Decision process | Who else, what timeline, what's the paper process |
| 24–28 | Mutual fit check | One honest disqualifier from each side |
| 28–30 | Next step | Specific, on-calendar, named attendees |

Write 3–4 open questions per section. Banned constructions: "are you currently struggling with…", "what keeps you up at night", "if I could show you a way to…". Buyers hear those 10 times a week and tune out.

### Phase 4 — Trap questions

Write 2 questions designed to surface deal-killers early: budget reality, competing initiative, internal incumbent. Better to find them in minute 12 than minute 28 of meeting four.

### Phase 5 — Pre-call mental model

In one sentence: what does the rep need the buyer to *feel* by minute 28 to want a second meeting? Write it down. If it's "convinced of ROI", the rep is pitching, not discovering. Try again.

## Hand-off

Output the one-page brief — account facts, buyer notes, the hypothesis, the timed agenda, the question script, and the two trap questions — formatted so the rep can read it on a phone in the lobby. End with the one disqualifier that, if surfaced, means the rep walks away clean.
