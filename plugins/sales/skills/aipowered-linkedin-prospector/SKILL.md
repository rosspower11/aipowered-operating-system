# LinkedIn Prospector

A skill for running LinkedIn outbound that gets replies from senior buyers — not just from other salespeople. Use this when email deliverability is shot, when the ICP lives on LinkedIn more than in inboxes, or when a rep needs a multi-touch DM cadence that doesn't read like a Sales Navigator template. The output is a connection note plus 4 follow-up DMs, branched by reply behavior.

## When to use this skill

- Targeting VP+ buyers who ignore cold email but post on LinkedIn weekly
- Email domain is burned and the team needs a non-email channel that still pulls meetings
- Building a founder-led social-selling motion (founder posts, rep DMs)
- Re-engaging a stalled list where prospects have viewed the rep's profile but never replied
- Running a niche ICP where Apollo data is sparse but LinkedIn coverage is dense

## When NOT to use this skill

- The buyer is a developer, ops manager, or anyone who hates LinkedIn (talk to them where they live)
- For high-volume SMB outbound where 1:1 DMs don't pencil — use email
- When the rep's own LinkedIn profile looks like a resume, not a buyer-relevant story (fix the profile first)

## How to run

### Phase 1 — Profile audit (gate the cadence on this)

Before writing a single DM, check the sender's profile. Headline must name the buyer's pain, not the rep's title. Banner and featured section must show proof (case study, podcast, customer logo). If the profile reads "Account Executive at [Company]", stop and rewrite it — DMs from a generic profile get archived. Confirm the profile is buyer-ready before continuing.

### Phase 2 — Connection note

One sentence. ≤200 characters. No "I'd love to connect." Reference one specific thing on their profile or a recent post. Never mention the offer in the connection note — that's what gets you blocked. Two variants: one for posters (reference the post), one for non-posters (reference the role + a real trigger).

### Phase 3 — The 4-touch cadence

| Touch | Day after accept | Format | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | DM | Soft open — question, no link, no calendar |
| 2 | 5 | Voice note (≤45s) | Pattern interrupt — most reps don't do this |
| 3 | 10 | DM | Proof drop — one customer, one number, one sentence |
| 4 | 18 | DM | Break-up — "wrong time? happy to circle back in Q3" |

Rules:
- No Calendly link before touch 3. Asking for a meeting in DM 1 is why the reply rate is 0.
- Voice notes outperform text 2–3x at VP+ level. Use them.
- Every touch ≤60 words written, ≤45 seconds spoken.
- Never quote their own job description back at them. They wrote it; they know what's in it.

### Phase 4 — Reply branching

Three reply types, three responses prepped in advance:

- **"Tell me more"** → answer the question, then one tight CTA (15-min walk-through)
- **"Not the right person"** → ask for the warm intro by name, not by title
- **"Not now"** → log the date they said, set a calendar trigger, move on

### Phase 5 — Anti-patterns to flag

Ban these phrases in the output: "hop on a quick call", "pick your brain", "synergy", "thought leader", "exciting opportunity". If the draft contains any, rewrite.

## Hand-off

Deliver as a single doc the rep can paste into Sales Navigator or HeyReach: connection note (2 variants), 4 DMs with day offsets, voice-note script, and the 3-branch reply playbook. End with the one custom field (trigger) the rep needs to fill in per prospect — nothing else should change between sends.
