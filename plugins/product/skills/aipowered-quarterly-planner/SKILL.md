# Quarterly Planner

A skill for running quarterly planning without the four-day off-site. Use this when the company is small enough that a structured async cycle beats a calendar war, but big enough that "what are we doing this quarter?" needs more than one person's gut. The output is a sequenced plan with named trade-offs, owner per bet, and a North Star metric the quarter will move.

## When to use this skill

- A new quarter starts in two to four weeks and the inputs are scattered across docs
- Three teams need to commit to interlocking work and currently aren't aligned
- A founder wants to plan a quarter without losing a week of build time
- An OKR cycle is being run and the leadership team wants to compress the calendar

## When NOT to use this skill

- For monthly or sprint planning — too much process for the cadence
- When the strategy is genuinely unsettled — planning a quarter on a missing thesis just postpones the strategy fight
- For solo founders pre-PMF — write a one-pager, not a plan

## How to run

### Phase 1 — Inputs (week minus 2)

Collect, don't debate. Five inputs, one document each, max one page:

1. **Last quarter retrospective** — what shipped, what didn't, what we learned. Not a status report.
2. **Customer signal** — top three themes from research, support, sales calls. Verbatim where possible.
3. **Metric review** — what moved, what didn't, where the North Star sits.
4. **Bottoms-up requests** — every team's top three asks, costed roughly.
5. **Strategic constraints** — runway, hiring, partnerships, regulation. The non-negotiables.

Send all five to participants 5 days before the synthesis session. No meetings yet.

### Phase 2 — Bet shortlist (week minus 1)

A small group (founder + 2 to 4 leads) writes a shortlist of 6 to 10 candidate bets. Each bet is a sentence: a customer, an outcome, and a rough cost. Score each against the North Star metric — does this move it, indirectly support it, or is it unrelated. Unrelated bets need a separate justification.

### Phase 3 — Trade-off session (60 to 90 minutes, sync)

This is the only sync meeting in the cycle. Walk the shortlist. For each bet, three questions: do we believe the impact estimate, do we have the team to ship it, what does it crowd out. Cut to the top 3 to 5 bets the company can actually deliver. Anything that survives is the plan.

### Phase 4 — Sequencing

Lay the surviving bets across the quarter in weeks. Identify the dependencies; pull the blocked ones earlier. Resist the urge to start everything in week 1 — front-loaded plans miss because no one shipped the second wave.

### Phase 5 — Trade-offs memo

The most important artefact. One page: what we're not doing this quarter, who it disappoints, and what would change our mind. This is the document that prevents Q+1 from being a relitigation of Q.

### Phase 6 — Commitment ritual

Each bet gets one named owner. Not a team — a person. The owner writes a 200-word commit memo: definition of done, the metric, the first two weeks of work. These memos go in a shared doc, week 1 of the quarter.

## Hand-off

Deliver the shortlist, the sequenced plan, the trade-offs memo, and the commit memos as one packet. Offer to run a 30-minute company-wide read-out at the start of the quarter — not a presentation, a Q&A on the doc people have already read.
